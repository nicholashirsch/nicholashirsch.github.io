sbms

I had the privilege of working on Saber Astronautics' Space Battle Management System (SBMS). While I was there I wrote a UDL- compliant conjunction assessment service capable of scanning the entire tracked catalog for potential collisions. I implemented a complete C++/Java pipeline including prescreening and probability of collision calculation which meets TRACCS and SDS 18 guidelines. I also utilized Docker to deploy a containerized version of this code to Saber's Kubernetes stack.

C++ · Java · Kubernetes · Docker · Conjunction Assessment

hohmannpy

I got tired of Orekit turning all my repos into a multi-language mess - so I built my own open-source astrodynamics library native in Python. This is a distillation of all my astrodynamics knowledge down into a single, highly optimized, core. HohmannPy features a variety of propagation schemes, a robust suite of perturbations (non-spherical gravity, solar radiation, etc.), various orbit determination methods (including Gibbs and Lagrange), quaternion-based attitude propagation, and more. All of this is coupled with intelligent wiring which allows constellations involving thousands of satellites to be simulated for months at a time. In addition, I created the HohmannPy Viewer using Qt so that users can visualize their simulations with only a single additional line of code.

Python · Astrodynamics

uocs

I like optimal control, but even when using direct transcription methods it can be tedious to design and tune the controller. As such, I created UOCS: the “universal optimal control software”. UOCS uses Lagrange polynomials as its basis functions in a method akin to a neural network. As such, the user only needs to supply three things: the plant dynamics, cost function, and relevant constraints. UOCS handles the rest and spits out the discrete control law and trajectory. This was the first project I completed in college, and also what taught me to code; but even now I think it holds its own in my portfolio. Oh, and I open sourced it.

MATLAB · Optimal Control

skipper

When I first joined Florida Rocket Lab we hadn't even hot fired our first engine, let alone begun thinking about putting one on a flight vehicle. However, even back then I knew we'd need GNC experience if that was ever going to be a reality. As such I founded FRL's first GNC team and began leading work on Skipper: a mono-propelled VTOL drone designed as FRL's GNC and avionics test bed. Skipper was designed to have dynamics modeling a TVC rocket and I derived the equations of motion from the ground-up to ensure this was the case. I then designed Skipper's flight controller, going through a series of iterations before landing on a linear- quadratic integrator + extended Kalman filter combination designed for both stable hovering and in-flight reference tracking. Prototyping took place in MATLAB + Simulink and deployment utilized C++/Arduino on a Teensy Nano.

MATLAB · Simulink · C++

energy-balance-model

One summer I got bored, so I built my own hyper-realistic planetary climate simulator. The climate is 100% emergent, everything: biome distribution, temperature/humidity profiles, and more appears dynamically over the course of a simulation. I made this to challenge myself, I wanted to take a subject I knew nothing about and see if I could make a functional prototype out of it in only a few months. Climate simulation involves a lot of PDEs, so I prioritized parallelization and numerical stability so that I could simulate hundreds of years in minutes. In addition, in the process of building this project I built a small CFD solver from scratch to model atmospheric circulation.

C# · Multithreading · CFD
